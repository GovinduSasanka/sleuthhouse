Rainy day activities for kids: turn your house into a crime scene

When it’s pouring outside and everyone is bouncing off the walls, give that energy a job. Here’s a 10-minute setup that becomes an hour of running, reading and reasoning, plus eight more ideas for the rest of the day.

The big one: an indoor detective mystery

Rainy days need something that gets kids moving and thinking. A mystery hunt does both. Clue cards are hidden around the house, each clue rules out a suspect, and each card has a riddle or code leading to the next hiding spot.

What makes it work at home is making it personal. With Sleuthhouse, the suspects are your real family, pets and toys, and the clues hide in your real rooms. Kids get very invested when the evidence points at the cat.

Setup in 10 minutes

  1. Open the case builder and type in the detectives, suspects and rooms.
  2. Print the kit. Black and white is fine.
  3. Cut out the clue cards and hide them where the grown-ups’ sheet says.
  4. Read the mission letter out loud in your most serious voice.

Make it fit your day

  • Siblings who fight? Put them on the same team against the clock instead of against each other.
  • Different ages? Choose the level for the youngest, and let the older child be the code-breaker.
  • Only have 30 minutes? Rookie cases use 3–5 clue cards. Master cases can fill an entire afternoon.
  • Grandparents visiting? Make Grandpa the prime suspect. Everyone wins.

8 more screen-free rainy day ideas

  1. Blanket fort HQ. Build a fort first, and it becomes the detectives’ headquarters for the mystery.
  2. Bake the evidence. Make cookies together, then play The Case of the Vanished Cookies while they cool.
  3. Invisible ink letters. Write with lemon juice on paper; a grown-up warms it with a hairdryer to reveal the message.
  4. Sock-puppet interrogation. Kids make puppets of the suspects and act out the confession afterwards.
  5. Indoor obstacle course. Cushions, chairs and a “laser maze” of string or wool across the hallway.
  6. Fingerprint art. An ink pad and paper. Turn each thumbprint into an animal or a tiny detective.
  7. Draw a map of the house. Kids draw their home from above, then mark where each clue was found.
  8. Write your own riddle. Challenge kids to write a riddle for a hiding spot, and hide something for a grown-up to find.

Why mysteries are good for kids

Beyond keeping the peace, a mystery hunt practises reading for detail (every word in a clue matters), logical elimination (if the thief wore a hat and Dad didn’t, Dad is cleared) and teamwork. Codes like emoji substitution and pigpen are real cipher systems, which is a sneaky bit of maths and pattern-spotting.

Questions

Do we need any special supplies?

Just a printer, scissors and a pencil. A torch and a magnifying glass make it more fun but aren’t required.

Is it really screen-free?

Yes. You use a screen for a few minutes to build and print the case. After that, it’s all paper, running around and thinking.

How long does it take?

About 10 minutes to set up and 20–75 minutes to play, depending on the level.
