A Halloween mystery game for kids, set in your own house

Somebody has stolen the Halloween candy. The suspects are Mum, Dad, Grandma and the dog. The clues are hidden under your own sofa cushions. Here’s how to run the Great Halloween Candy Heist at home in one evening.

Why a mystery works so well on Halloween

Halloween nights are busy but oddly long. The costumes go on early, trick-or-treating ends quickly, and then there’s a sugar-fuelled hour before bed. A detective game fills that hour with running between rooms, reading clues out loud and arguing about who did it. At the end, everyone gets the candy back.

It also fits the season. There’s a mystery, dim lights and a “villain”, but nothing actually frightening. The worst thing that happens is Grandma confessing that the candy “looked lonely”.

How the Great Halloween Candy Heist works

  1. You add your people and rooms. Enter the detectives’ names, 3–8 suspects (family, pets and toys all count) and the rooms in your home.
  2. Sleuthhouse builds a unique case. A logic engine decides secretly what happened, then writes clues so that exactly one suspect can be the thief. No card gives it away early.
  3. You print and hide the clue cards. The grown-ups’ sheet lists exactly where each card goes, like “in the umbrella stand” or “under the doormat”.
  4. Detectives hunt. Each card has a true clue about the thief and a riddle or code leading to the next hiding spot.
  5. The accusation. Detectives name the thief, and the guilty party reads their confession in a spooky voice.

Setting the mood (spooky, not scary)

  • Lights low, torches on. Hand each detective a flashlight and turn off the big lights. Finding a clue card in a torch beam feels much more exciting.
  • Costumes become disguises. Whatever they wore trick-or-treating, they’re now undercover detectives.
  • Evidence bags. Zip-top sandwich bags labelled “EVIDENCE” make every clue card feel official.
  • A crime scene. Leave the empty candy bucket where the grown-ups’ sheet says the crime happened, with a single wrapper next to it.
  • A dramatic confession. Whoever the thief turns out to be should read the confession in their best vampire voice.

Mixed ages? Pick the level for the youngest detective and let older kids be “senior partners” who read the clues aloud.

Running it at a Halloween party

With several kids, switch on party mode. Detectives split into Team Red and Team Blue, and every hiding spot gets one card for each team. Both teams chase the same thief, and the first team to make a correct accusation wins. Wrong accusations don’t end the game; the team just keeps investigating. That keeps it friendly.

A 10-minute setup checklist

  • Print the kit (black and white is fine) and keep the grown-ups’ sheet and solution page private.
  • Cut out the suspect cards and clue cards.
  • Hide the clue cards in the listed spots. Fold them so they’re easy to spot but not obvious.
  • Put the mission letter somewhere dramatic, like on the empty candy bucket.
  • Hide the actual candy somewhere only you know, for the happy ending.

Questions

Is it scary?

No. Nobody gets hurt: the crime is stolen candy, the suspects are your own family, and the thief reads out a silly confession at the end.

Can we play again next year?

Yes. Every case number creates a brand-new mystery with a different thief, clues and hiding spots.

Do the grown-up suspects need to act?

Only if they want to! Suspects don’t need to do anything during the hunt, but it’s fun if they look very innocent whenever a detective walks past.

How long does it take to set up?

About 10 minutes to print, cut and hide the cards.
